AmpliTrim

Choose DADA2 truncation lengths and maxEE from your own reads.

AmpliTrim is for Illumina paired-end amplicon reads (tested on 16S and ITS data from MiSeq and NovaSeq runs). AmpliTrim tests candidate settings on your own reads, recommends the best, and shows why. It does not change your files: it only recommends settings.

Quick start How it works View on GitHub User guide

The problem

In DADA2 you pick truncLenF, truncLenR and maxEE. Cut too short, and your longest amplicons cannot merge. Cut too long, and noisy read ends cost you reads. Most people choose by eye from quality plots.

  1. FASTQ→
  2. cutadapt→
  3. AmpliTrim→
  4. DADA2

Run AmpliTrim after primer removal and before DADA2 (in R or in QIIME 2). It needs paired reads (R1 and R2), not long reads or single-end data.

Why use it

  • It uses every read pair.

    After primer removal, reads often differ in length. AmpliTrim does not need them equal, so nothing is thrown away.

  • It predicts the result.

    It replicates DADA2's quality filter exactly and merges a sample of your pairs with vsearch, so you see how many will pass and merge.

  • It reads your data, not a table.

    It measures your amplicon lengths, checks that your longest amplicons can merge, and handles ITS and other regions where lengths vary.

  • It is ready to use.

    You get copy-paste settings for DADA2, in R or in QIIME 2's dada2 denoise-paired, and a stable summary.json for pipelines.

Quick start

pip install .      # Python 3.9 or later; vsearch must also be on your PATH
amplitrim --input-dir reads/ --work-dir work --out-dir report

Example output (from simulated reads; your numbers will differ):

AmpliTrim 0.1.0
Analysing reads...

Recommended DADA2 settings
  truncLenF = 231   truncLenR = 217   maxEE = 2   minOverlap = 12

  99.7% of the 12,000 read pairs analysed should pass filtering and merge.
  Covers amplicons up to 436 nt (your longest: 434 nt).

Report: report/REPORT.md

report/REPORT.md has the settings with copy-paste snippets for DADA2 in R and in QIIME 2's dada2 denoise-paired, what to expect, and anything worth checking. To try it without your own data, make a small simulated dataset (from a clone of this repository; the generator needs numpy):

python tests/simulate.py demo --samples 2 --pairs 1500 --seed 11
amplitrim --input-dir demo/primer_trimmed --work-dir work --out-dir out

These reads are simulated, so use them to see what the report looks like, not to judge the tool. Expect lengths that cover the longest amplicon (434 nt), a maxEE of 2, and more than 70% of the pairs passing and merging.

What you need

  • Python 3.9 or later and vsearch on your PATH (install steps, including Windows).
  • Paired FASTQ files in one folder, named like sample_R1.fastq.gz and sample_R2.fastq.gz.
  • Primers and adapters already removed (for example with cutadapt), and no quality trimming. R1 must be the forward read (details).

Options

Required: --input-dir, --work-dir and --out-dir. Optional: --its (for amplicons that vary a lot in length, such as ITS) and --verbose. AmpliTrim chooses everything else, including maxEE (user guide).

How the settings are chosen

SettingHow
truncLenF, truncLenRThe shortest total that can merge your longest amplicons (the amplicon plus 12), split between the two reads where quality allows. Real merges are tested with vsearch
maxEE2 (DADA2's default) for clean reads. For noisy reads, a limit for each read that covers three quarters of its reads, never below 2, with a note if it goes above 5 (how)
minOverlap12 (DADA2's default)

The outputs are REPORT.md (read this one), summary.json (for scripts; every key) and, if matplotlib is installed (pip install ".[plot]"), summary.png (a figure of your amplicon lengths and how errors build up).

Limits

  • It is for DADA2. QIIME 2's other denoiser, Deblur, uses different settings (one trim length for merged reads), which AmpliTrim does not recommend (guide).
  • The yield is an optimistic estimate. It is measured before DADA2 denoises the reads. Use it to compare settings, and run DADA2 once to confirm.
  • It checks quality and mergeability, not accuracy.
  • A tail of much longer amplicons (for example non-target 18S) can force long, noisy reads. Only the longest 0.2% are set aside, so remove such reads first (guide).
